Built for how room-by-room properties actually run.

Every room, every tenancy, one clear view.

Properties hold rooms, rooms hold tenancies, tenancies hold the people living in them — so a shared kitchen, a couple splitting one room, or a short weekend stay all fit the same structure. See what's occupied, what's vacant and what's changing this week without maintaining a separate spreadsheet for every building.

Every room, every tenancy, one clear view.

Properties hold rooms, rooms hold tenancies, tenancies hold the people living in them — so a shared kitchen, a couple splitting one room, or a short weekend stay all fit the same structure. See what's occupied, what's vacant and what's changing this week without maintaining a separate spreadsheet for every building.

Prove the condition of a room, rather than remember it.

Each room is broken into inspected sections — walls, fixtures, appliances, furniture — rated for condition and cleanliness, with dated photos attached to every item. Run the same checklist at move-in and move-out and Roomly builds the comparison automatically, so you can show exactly what changed rather than argue about it.

See what came in, what went out, and what it left you.

Rent due dates, arrears and chasing are tracked automatically, and every cost a property generates — a repair, a purchase, a utility bill — lands in one expense ledger, totalled by UK tax year. Bills-included rent gets checked against what utilities actually cost, so a room quietly losing money shows up rather than staying buried in the total.

Decisions behind the build

Decisions behind the build

Four choices that shaped how Roomly works, and the reasoning behind each one.

  • Colour marks something a person has to act on — never a category. Two tiles on the dashboard carry a tone, overdue rent and missing documents, and both go grey at zero. Six coloured cards is six things shouting, so nothing reads as urgent.

    Dashboard

    Why the interface is mostly black on white

    Colour marks something a person has to act on — never a category. Two tiles on the dashboard carry a tone, overdue rent and missing documents, and both go grey at zero. Six coloured cards is six things shouting, so nothing reads as urgent.

    Dashboard

    Why the interface is mostly black on white

  • Check-in and check-out are the same structure recorded twice against one tenancy, which makes the comparison a straightforward join — and makes “this was Good in January and is Poor now” provable rather than remembered.

    Inventory

    Why condition is recorded the same way twice

    Check-in and check-out are the same structure recorded twice against one tenancy, which makes the comparison a straightforward join — and makes “this was Good in January and is Poor now” provable rather than remembered.

    Inventory

    Why condition is recorded the same way twice

  • Rent alerts deliberately fire the day after the due date. The tenant gets their full due day to pay before anyone is chased, and the reminder goes out in the language they actually read.

    Rent

    Why chasing starts a day late, on purpose

    Rent alerts deliberately fire the day after the due date. The tenant gets their full due day to pay before anyone is chased, and the reminder goes out in the language they actually read.

    Rent

    Why chasing starts a day late, on purpose

  • Identity documents are destroyed a year after the tenancy ends and money records kept for six, because that is what the law asks for. Retention is enforced by a job that deletes, not a policy page that promises.
